# House voice — how the owner actually writes

Every line below is real. They come from the 71 staff takeovers in
`exports/conversations.jsonl` (tagged `[MANUAL FOLLOW-UP]`), the moments a
human stepped in because the bot was not doing the job. That makes them the
best sample we have of how this business talks to its own customers.

Write bot copy that sounds like this. Not like the bot.

## The short version

Short. Plain words. One question. No wind-up.

## What they actually sent

Asking for what they need:

- "what time?"
- "Ko murikupi?"
- "All good, what area are you in"
- "When were you hoping to get this done?"
- "What I meant to ask was, when were you hoping to get this done?"
- "or rather when would be available?"
- "what exactly did you want done in your garage"
- "Do you maybe have a picture of the bathroom you want to put the tub"
- "What's the best email to reach you on?"

Saying sorry:

- "sorry, did you?"
- "Sorry could you please type"
- "Sorry, I meant to ask where are you located"
- "so sorry about that, that text was meant for another client..."

Saying what we do and do not do:

- "Plumber, we do supply and install only"
- "We are supply and install only we do not supply without install"
- "Site assessments are free of charge"

Closing:

- "We can come and see the place then tell how we can move forward from
  there, is that fair for you?"
- "All good, we'll speak on Thursday then..."
- "Got it, no problem."
- "Great I see it, what's the best email to reach you on?"

## The rules these lines follow

1. **Short.** Most are under twelve words. "what time?" is a whole message.
2. **Plain words.** "come and see the place", never "conduct an on-site
   assessment". "what exactly do you want done", never "could you elaborate on
   the scope".
3. **One question, asked straight.** No preamble, no softening clause in front
   of it.
4. **Sorry is one word.** "sorry, did you?" Not "I apologise for any
   confusion caused."
5. **Shona when the lead uses Shona**, mixed the way people really mix it:
   "Ko murikupi?", "kunyange picture wenzvimbo yacho anoshanda", "Network
   yanga ya dakwa".
6. **No emojis. No dashes.** Check this against the source yourself: every
   emoji and every em dash in that export sits inside a block the staff member
   PASTED from the bot. Nothing a human typed has either.
7. **Limits are stated flat.** "we do not supply without install". No cushion
   around it.
8. **Soft closes are plain.** "is that fair for you?" does the work that "would
   that be agreeable to you?" only pretends to.

## How to tell you have drifted

The long, tidy, bulleted messages in that export are staff pasting bot copy,
not writing. If a draft has a bulleted list, an em dash, a "Just a quick note",
or a sentence that sets up the next sentence, it is bot copy wearing a hat.

Read the draft out loud. If it sounds like something a busy plumber would
thumb into a phone between jobs, it is right. If it sounds like a brochure, cut
it until it does.
