SiteVisitReport, the quote follow-up sequence, PlanQuoteRequest and VisitProposal.

### Post-visit debrief + quote follow-ups (`bot/post_visit.py`)
- Everything after a site visit hangs off **one row, `SiteVisitReport`** (OneToOne on the lead, migration 0077), and **one tokenized URL**. Two entry points open that same URL: the **in-app banner pinned at the very top of the appointment detail screen** ("Is the site visit complete?" → `site_visit_start`, staff-only, creates the row then redirects). **It replaced the old `complete_site_visit` view, URL and template, which are deleted** — that button sat buried in the Scheduling card and wrote `site_visit_notes` / `plumber_assessment` with no quote and no follow-ups behind it. There is now ONE way to log a visit. The banner is **always rendered and never gated on the row looking tidy** (not on `status='confirmed'`, not on a past slot): plenty of real visits happen without the bot ever pinning a slot, and gating the only control on that made those impossible to log. `_site_visit_banner` returns `{'state': 'open'}` or `{'state': 'resolved', 'report': ...}` — once logged it reports the outcome and what it set running rather than vanishing, so it is never a dead control. `is_due_for_report` still gates the CRON (an unscheduled lead has no visit-end to measure 35 minutes from); only the UI is ungated and the **fallback email to the plumber 35 minutes after the visit ends** (`send_site_visit_form_email`). One URL and one `submitted_at` is what makes the form single-use ACROSS both paths: `apply_submission` is the only writer of that timestamp and refuses a second call, and the cron stops sending the fallback the moment it is set. The form is deliberately **public, token-only** (`/site-visit/<token>/`, added to `TenantMiddleware._EXEMPT_PREFIXES`) — the plumber taps it from an email on their phone with no session; the token is a uuid4 and the row is single-use, so the link is the credential. Anything needing real authority (creating the quote) redirects to a staff-gated screen.
- **The banner lives INSIDE the Details tab and is NOT pinned** (owner rule, 2026-09-14, superseding the 2026-09-09 "above the tabs" arrangement). It sat above the tab bar so it was reachable whichever tab you were reading, but a banner about the SITE VISIT then held the top of the Quotes, WhatsApp and Email tabs, over content it has nothing to do with - and being outside the only scrolling box on the screen, it never moved off them. It is now the first child of `#tab-details`, which scrolls its own content, so it is still the first thing seen (Details is the tab a record opens on) and it scrolls away like anything else on a page. **The whole `is-flow-scroll` mechanism is gone** - the pane-level scroller, the id-spelled overrides, the sticky `.appt-tabbar` and the `showAppointmentTab` class toggle - because it existed only to unpin a banner that no longer sits there; each pane scrolls itself again and the tab bar stays visible by sitting above them. Do not reintroduce a pane-level scroller to make the banner "reachable from every tab": that is the arrangement this replaced. Pinned by `VisitBannerLivesInTheDetailsTabTests` (bot/test_views_actions.py) and `BannerLivesInTheDetailsTabTests` (bot/test_phone_quote.py). (Note `{# #}` cannot span lines in a Django template - `TemplateCommentTests` catches it.)
- **The outcome radio is the gate.** Only `went_ahead` reveals the questions and the Create Quote button; `no_show` / `rescheduled` / `not_proceeding` submit straight through to `_route_other_outcome` (no quote, no sequence, `sequence='stopped'`) and write the lead state the rest of the dashboard reads — `no_show` sets status, `rescheduled` returns it to pending for the reschedule screen, `not_proceeding` sets `is_lead_active=False`, which is the flag every follow-up path already honours. `went_ahead` requires an email (the form is the ONLY place a missing one can be added) and marks the visit complete on the lead.
- **Job notes carry into the quote screen.** `CreateQuotationView` puts the report's notes in `quote_prefill_notes`, and `create_quotation.html` renders them with `data-prefilled="1"` — `loadAppointmentData` checks that flag before writing `projectNotes`, or its `project_description` fetch would blank what the plumber typed on site. The sectioned layout does not take them: its `notes` are the payment terms, not a description.
- **Send is on the EDITOR and independent of Save.** Both buttons are always enabled; each runs `persist()` first (save, then send), so the plumber never has to press Save and what the customer gets is the quote on screen. `currentQuotationId` is filled by the first save, so a second send updates the same row rather than raising another (Download and Delete follow it too).
- **The PDF follows the TENANT's layout, like every screen does** (`bot/quote_pdf.py`). One renderer served everybody, so a sectioned tenant's customers received a flat list with none of their sections, subtotals, VAT, terms, banking or signature line — a document nothing like the one their plumber approved. `build_quotation_pdf` now branches on the same `is_sectioned(tenant)` the screens use: `_flat_sheet` mirrors `quote_flat_document.html`, `_sectioned_sheet` mirrors `quote_sectioned_view.html` + `quote_footer.html`. The sectioned renderer takes its colours and proportions straight from `quote_sectioned_css.html` (`SEC` / `SEC_COLS`): a **two-column letterhead** (mark and name left in `--bq-blue`, contacts right in `--bq-teal`), the green `t / a` rule, a centred red underlined "Quotation", and a green-ruled table led by **QTY | DESCRIPTION | UNIT PRICE | TOTAL PRICE** — the sheet's own order, not Item-first. It is **ONE table** with the section headings as rows, so the column header is drawn once and repeats only on a page break. Text wraps to a MEASURED width (`_wrap_px`, via `canvas.stringWidth`): a character count put Barmak's services blurb past the right margin, where the customer's copy cut it mid-word. Markup cannot be shared between HTML and a reportlab canvas, so `QuotePdfMatchesTheAppTests` asserts the FIGURES and blocks agree, and **`QuotePdfGeometryTests` asserts WHERE things land** — name left, contacts right, columns in order, header drawn once, nothing past the right margin — by reading the drawing coordinates out of the content stream (which reportlab writes ASCII85 **and** flate-compressed, so both layers come off first). It also fixes a hardcoded `US$` on every tenant's figures, and prints the ITEM LINES rather than the double-counted `materials_cost` column.
- **`strapline` was never a letterhead key.** `TenantConfig.letterhead()` exposes `services_blurb` / `maintenance_blurb` / `tagline`; the flat letterhead partial and the PDF both asked for `lh.strapline`, so that line silently never rendered. Read the schema in `letterhead()` before adding a key to a quote document.
- **The PDF really is attached, via the Web Share API.** `navigator.share({files:[pdf]})` hands WhatsApp the actual file; the plumber picks the chat and sends. Two routes, because the platform gives us two and neither does everything: **share preselects the FILE but the OS owns the contact picker**, and **a wa.me link preselects the CHAT but no link of any kind can carry a file**. Both are offered, whichever the device supports leading. The blob is fetched on page LOAD, not on click — `navigator.share` must run inside the gesture that triggered it, and awaiting a fetch first spends that gesture (Safari throws `NotAllowedError`). The button stays `hidden` until `canShare({files})` passes against the REAL file, so a desktop never gets a control that throws on tap; an `AbortError` (share sheet closed) is not reported as a failure. Exercised in Node against both device shapes.
- **Every WhatsApp send control in the app is `quotation_whatsapp_handoff`** — the quotes list, both quote pages, the appointment's Quotes tab (which emails from the same card), and the editor (which saves, then points a tab opened inside the click at it). It downloads the PDF, shows the message and opens the chat on the plumber's own number; **the bot never sends the quote**. `send_quotation` (the Cloud API document send) still works but is deliberately linked from no screen.
- **WhatsApp hands off to the plumber's OWN app.** The button downloads the PDF and opens `wa.me/<lead digits>` with a prefilled line, so the quote comes from the number the customer already knows and the **24h messaging window that limits the bot's own sends does not apply** (a quote often goes out days after the visit, when a Cloud API document send would 131047). **A wa.me link and a `mailto:` draft carry TEXT only — neither can attach a file** — which is why the PDF is downloaded first, via a hidden iframe so the page stays put. The handoff window is opened synchronously inside the click and pointed at the URL after the save resolves, or the popup blocker eats it.
- **Email offers both routes every time**, with the tenant's `TenantProfile.quote_email_mode` (migration 0080, set on the Profile page) deciding only which is pre-marked `Default`: `platform` sends now from the configured address with the PDF attached, `manual` downloads the PDF and opens a `mailto:` draft in the plumber's own app. The choice is a default, never a restriction.
- **A prefilled message is a CLOSE, not a covering note** (`bot/lead_handoff.py`). The quote handoff went out as "here is your quote for bathroom renovation." and asked for nothing, so its best possible outcome was a lead who read the PDF and did nothing. A quote IS the offer, so `build_quote_message` / `quote_message_body` say why the document is worth opening and then ask for a DAY, never for a decision: "If you are happy with it I can get you booked in. Which suits you better, earlier in the week or later on?" **ONE builder feeds the WhatsApp handoff and BOTH editors' email drafts** - the editors take the BODY from the server and build only the greeting, because a standalone quote's client name is typed on the sheet and exists on no row. It carries **no figure** (the total is in the PDF; a chat line that later disagrees with the document is worse than no number) and **no claim about what the price covers** - "fixed", "all in", "no extras", free, guarantee, deposit are one tenant's USPs and this text reaches another tenant's customers. Pinned by `QuoteMessageIsTheCloseTests`. **The `hi + '

' + BODY` join must be an ESCAPED newline**: written as a real one it closes the JS literal and kills the whole editor handler on a page still returning 200 - no Django assertion sees it, `node --check` on the dumped pages does.
- **The lead draft's ask is their outcome, with a destination** (`build_ask`). Three changes, all Hormozi: the opener is the price they are waiting on rather than our paperwork ("So I can get this priced properly for you" not "To give you an accurate quote, I'll need"); the COUNT is said out loud (`_count_phrase` - "a few more things" could be three or seven, and a lead who cannot see the end of a request puts it off); and `_ask_payoff` closes the list with what happens once they answer, which is the price itself when a plan is on file and the look first when there is none. That payoff is a STATEMENT on purpose - **a question after a list is the question they answer instead of the list** - and it names no cost, because some tenants charge for the visit. `_recap` ends on "Let me know if I have anything wrong", the cheapest possible yes, rather than a bare receipt. Copy pairs in `.claude/skills/plumbot-sales-flow/references/corrected-examples.md`.
- **`mark_quotation_sent` records a handoff.** The server never sees a message sent from the plumber's own app, so without it the quotes list would read "Not sent" forever on those routes. It records what the plumber DID, and the conversation note says "handed to the plumber to send" rather than claiming delivery.
- **The lead's Quotes tab sends on both channels.** It offered WhatsApp only, so the one card a plumber reaches from the conversation could not email a quote - the channel that always arrives, since a quote usually goes out days after the visit with the lead's 24h WhatsApp window shut. Email sits beside the handoff there now, with the quotes list's own rules: `Resend email` once `sent_via_email`, and no address on file becomes a link to the editor (`target="_top"` - the editor is a whole page, and the tab is normally read inside the conversations workspace iframe) rather than a dead button. The card's Sent line reads `sent_channels` / `sent_channel_label` instead of `sent_via_whatsapp` alone, which showed nothing for a quote emailed from that very card. **`send_quotation_email` honours a validated `next`** (`safe_return_path`, the POST counterpart of `quote_return_url` - only the local PATH survives, so a value that reached the form cannot redirect off-site), because its default landing is the quote's own view page and a full page rendered into that narrow pane strands the plumber away from the chat they were reading; the tab's `next` is `quotes_tab_url`, built in `AppointmentDetailView` with **`tab` forced to `quotes`** (the workspace switches panes by calling `showAppointmentTab()` in the frame, which never touches the URL, so `?tab=` can still say `details`) and every other flag carried through. The quotes list passes its own full path, so emailing from page 3 of a search comes back to page 3 of that search. Pinned by `LeadQuotesTabSendTests`.
- **The two quote-send buttons are independent.** `send_quotation` (WhatsApp, `sent_via_whatsapp`) and `send_quotation_email` (email + the same `build_quotation_pdf_file` PDF, `sent_via_email`) each keep their own flag, neither is hidden after use, and **neither has any bearing on the follow-up channel, which is always email** — email is the address the form always captures, so it is the only reliable automated channel.
- **The backlog guard is what makes this safe to switch on** (`POST_VISIT_BACKLOG_DAYS`, default 3). This machine has NEVER run in production: the command shipped but was never added to `PLUMBOT_CRON`, so enabling it cold fired 26 emails about visits going back months (14 form links, 12 no-email handbacks) - which is how a plumber learns these emails are noise. A finished visit whose report has DONE nothing is now left alone once it is that stale, so the live dry run went 26 -> 1 (the one being a visit that ended 11 hours earlier). It also covers every future outage: a cron dead for a week no longer empties its backlog into an inbox.
- **The guard keys off ACTIVITY, never existence** (`report_is_untouched`). The first version asked `if report is None` and did nothing at all against real data, because **`ensure_report` is also called from the DETAIL PAGE** so the banner has a link - there are 18 rows on the live DB, none submitted and none having sent anything, empty containers indistinguishable from an untouched lead except that `report is None` is False. A row that has sent something or been submitted is in flight and keeps ticking whatever the age: **Case A confirmations are keyed to the JOB date and can be weeks out, and Case B's third ask lands ~11 days after the visit**, so an age filter on the tick's queryset would have stranded every sequence in flight. Pinned by `PostVisitBacklogGuardTests`.
- **`--dry-run` creates nothing, deliberately.** It used to call `ensure_report` before the dry-run check, so the command you would use to PREVIEW the backlog was the command that armed it - the guard gates opening, and once those rows existed every one would tick on the next real run. A dry run now evaluates against a transient unsaved report; two state corrections in `_send_confirmation` (fall back to the asks, mark done) were also writing through a dry run and are now guarded.
- **The scheduler is `run_post_visit_tick`** (command `send_post_visit_followups`, riding the existing `Email_Follow_Ups` service at */5). Three branches, all idempotent because every send is gated by the timestamp written as it goes out: **Case A** — a specific date on file gets ONE confirmation two days before at 09:00, then `sequence='done'`; **Case B** — a rough timeframe or "didn't say" gets ask #1 next day at noon, #2 three days later, #3 seven days after that, then cold + `mark_handed_off` + `send_post_visit_handback_email`; **Case C** — no form by noon the next day is treated as "no date, lead has email" and starts Case B directly, and with no email on file hands the lead back instead (once, gated by `no_email_notified_at`). **Case C leaves the form OPEN**, so its branch is re-entered every tick and must start the sequence exactly once — the `sequence != 'awaiting_form'` early-out is what stops the next tick resetting `next_action_at` and firing ask 2 minutes after ask 1.
- **A QUOTE BEING RAISED ARMS THE SAME SEQUENCE** (`start_quote_followups`, called from every quote-creation path: `create_quotation_api`, `create_standalone_quotation_api` and the legacy form view, beside `_mark_visit_complete_for_quote`). The asks had exactly ONE trigger, the debrief form, so a quote raised from the lead's screen, the quotes list or the standalone editor went out with nothing behind it - and the ask copy is already quote copy ("your quote should be with you now... roughly when were you hoping to have the work done?"), so this arms the existing machine from the other trigger rather than inventing a second one. Case B from noon the next day. Four refusals, all deliberate: a **synthetic key** (`quotation_only_` / `email_`) is not a lead; a **suppressed** lead is never chased; **no email means no arming**, because leaving the report awaiting the form is what keeps the no-email handback pointed at the plumber, where arming would silence it; and **the plumber's own answer outranks a quote** - only a report still `awaiting_form` is armed, so a second quote cannot restart a chase the lead has been through and a quote raised after "not proceeding" cannot talk over that decision. Two consequences elsewhere: `report_is_untouched` now counts an ARMED sequence as activity (or the backlog guard would throw away a chase armed on an older visit on the very next tick), and the 35-minute debrief email is skipped once a sequence is armed - it exists to get the flow moving, and a quote raised in the app has both answered its question and proved the plumber was there (Case C is unaffected: it arms at noon the next day, hours after that email has gone).
- **The tick looks at TWO querysets, `due_visits` and `armed_leads`** (`_leads_to_tick`, deduped by pk, debrief queryset first). `due_visits` finds a lead by their finished site visit, which is the wrong question once a sequence is running: a quote-armed lead may have no visit at all and would sit at its due moment forever. `armed_leads` is narrow on purpose - only `sequence in ('asks','confirm')` with a `next_action_at` that has passed - so it widens what the cron LOOKS at without widening what it may send.
- **`sequence_started_at` (migration 0091) is the anchor the cadence is measured from**, stamped once by every path that arms one. `created_at` cannot be it (the detail page opens a row the moment anybody views the lead, often weeks earlier) and `submitted_at` is null on both the paths that arm without the form, so the Email tab dated Case C's and every quote-armed lead's asks off the wrong moment. `projected_emails` reads `next_action_at` directly while ask 1 is still pending, which is what the cron will actually act on.
- **`lead_is_suppressed` is the single resolver for whether we may message a lead at all** — parked, handed-off, stopped, out-of-area, inactive, cancelled/completed, and job-already-booked. It is re-checked **before every customer-facing send**, not once at the top of the run: a lead can be parked or booked between two ticks.
- **`extract_expected_date` is deterministic and narrow on purpose.** A named day ("15/03", "20 October", "next Monday", "tomorrow") switches a lead from Case B to Case A via `record_lead_expected_date`; a rough answer ("in a few weeks", "sometime next month", "asap") must NOT match, or a confirmation goes out for a day nobody chose. `note_inbound_reply` is the hook, wired into `handle_text_message` and `process_inbound_emails`, and is a no-op for any lead without a live report.
- **`visit_end` does not trust `end_datetime` blindly.** `Appointment.save()` fills it once and never recomputes it, so a rescheduled visit carries the end time of the slot it used to hold; taking it at face value emailed the plumber a debrief for a visit that had not happened yet. A stale end (earlier than the start) falls back to `scheduled_datetime + duration`.
- **No follow-up renders a null date.** `build_post_visit_confirmation_email` raises rather than print a blank, and a confirm-branch report that somehow has no date falls back to the ask sequence instead of sending. The ask copy carries no date at all. The visit has already happened by the time these go out, so **the copy never re-pitches it** — "free", "site visit" and "no cost" are absent by test, which is the repeat-pitch rule applied to a new channel.
- **The Email tab lists EVERY email sequence on the lead, the PLUMBER's included.** `Appointment.get_upcoming_emails` covered two sequences out of five (delay re-engagement, appointment reminders), so the post-visit quote follow-ups, the plan-path chases and the visit check-ins were invisible on the one screen named after them - including the two emails the rest of the system waits on the plumber for: the debrief ("how did the visit go?") and the chases ("have you quoted this lead yet?"). A screen that names itself after a set and shows a third of it is worse than no screen, because it gets believed. **Each flow projects its OWN rows** - `post_visit.projected_emails`, `plan_quote.projected_emails`, `visit_proposal.projected_emails` - next to the offsets it schedules with and using the same helpers the cron schedules with (`next_day_noon`, `_at_local_hour`), so the screen cannot describe a cadence the cron does not run; `get_upcoming_emails` merges them, each in its own try, so a half-written report still shows the rows we CAN describe. Every row carries `to` ('customer' | 'plumber') and the card shows it as a chip. **Deliberately NOT a sixth tab** (owner decision, 2026-09-10): the tab bar splits by CHANNEL (WhatsApp / Email), so a tab for a recipient inside a channel would be two axes in one bar, and a sixth column squeezes the labels past legibility on a phone - `repeat(5, ...)` and the five-button count are asserted. `has_email` stays about the CUSTOMER's address and the copy says the plumber's rows are unaffected by its absence. Pinned by `EmailFollowupSectionTests`.

### Two product paths to a quote (`bot/plan_detection.py`, `bot/plan_quote.py`, `bot/visit_proposal.py`)
- **MEASURE path** (no plan): sell the paid site visit. **PLAN path** (a real plan arrives): the drawing carries the measurements, so there is nothing to measure up and the plumber quotes off it. `classify_media_kind` routes on the description vision already produced plus the MIME type — no extra call. Its middle case is the one that was missing: `_description_is_a_plan` rejects a lone "drawing" or "layout" because both describe photographs, and those used to fall through as photos with nobody asking; they now get one clarifier. A room description still beats a stray layout word. No `media_kind` column — `has_plan`/`plan_status` already carry it.
- **`PlanQuoteRequest`** (migration 0081, command `send_plan_quote_followups`): the plumber is emailed the job plus the plan an hour after it arrives, or as soon as description/area/timeline are in, whichever is first; chased at +2h/+4h/+8h until they answer the tokenized single-use form; then the lead is asked about the quote an hour after the plumber confirms (Branch A) or at +12h assuming it went out (Branch B, `quote_status='assumed'`). **Branch B waits for a contact window, Branch A does not** — B fires on a clock and +12h from a 3pm plan is 3am, while A is triggered by the plumber tapping the form, which is itself proof of working hours.
- **`VisitProposal`** (migration 0082, command `send_visit_checkins`): a future-dated MEASURE lead is penciled in for a real day near their target, then asked to confirm by email at target −7 and −4 days with a yes and a no link. **A lead with no email is handed to the PLUMBER** — those two check-ins go to them instead (mornings only), carrying a `wa.me` link with a message written ready to send, which starts a fresh 24h window we cannot open ourselves.
- **The tentative state is its own row, NOT a new `Appointment.status` value.** Every dashboard filter, the diary query and the follow-up eligibility read that field, and a fifth value would change what each of them counts. `apply_lead_answer` is the only place a future-dated visit becomes real, and it **writes the diary FIRST, then marks the row**: the other order leaves the row reading 'confirmed' with nothing booked and the check-ins stopped, while the lead is told it failed.
- All three flows follow `post_visit.py`: anchors and offsets rather than a job queue (there is no scheduler in this codebase), every send gated by the timestamp written as it goes out, lead state re-checked before EVERY send, and one bad lead never stopping the run. `plan_quote.lead_is_done` extends `lead_is_suppressed` rather than replacing it — a `status='confirmed'` lead with a slot ahead is the ordinary state after a site visit but means "they booked, stop" on the plan path.
- **The plumber is contacted by EMAIL, always** (owner rule, 2026-09-05). The media alert, plan alert, booking alert, job alert and scheduled reminders all email; the three WhatsApp briefings in `send_reminders` are off behind `PLUMBER_WHATSAPP_BRIEFINGS` because the email section below them already sends the same three things. Attachment MIME type is derived from the filename (`_attachment_type`) — two transports hardcoded `application/pdf`, and a plan is as often a photo of a drawing.
