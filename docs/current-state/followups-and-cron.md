send_followups (the cap, the floor, the owner scripts, placement), the Railway cron trap, and the staff stop/cancel controls.

### Follow-ups & cron (`bot/management/commands/`)
- `send_followups` — **FOUR is a CEILING, and FOUR HOURS is a FLOOR** (owner rule, 2026-09-09, superseding "four for every lead" of 2026-09-03 where the two collide). Three rules, in one place each: (1) at most `FOLLOWUP_CAP_PER_REPLY` = 4 touches between one message of the lead's and the next, counted by `touches_since_last_reply` off the TRANSCRIPT rather than any one loop's counter (each loop keeps its own — `followup_count`, and the two nudge states in `internal_notes` — and none can see the others, so a lead chased four times and then parked could be nudged four more times off the same silence; a `[MANUAL FOLLOW-UP]` by a human is deliberately not counted, since the cap is about the machine talking over itself). It is asked FIRST in `_is_ready_for_followup` and again in both nudge loops. (2) `FOLLOWUP_MIN_GAP_HOURS` = 4.0, and `LAST_CALL_MIN_GAP_HOURS` now EQUALS it — the old 0.75h last-call relaxation is gone, because a minimum is a minimum. `space_offsets` is the single spacing resolver, shared by the main schedule and both nudge loops: it pushes a close pair apart and **DROPS what no longer fits rather than squeezing it**, so when the gap and the count cannot both hold, the COUNT gives. (3) Every touch is contextual — see the fallback templates below. **THE CONSEQUENCE:** with the old two blocks (~5.5 sendable hours) a standard 24h lead got 1 to 3 touches, usually 2. `CONTACT_WINDOWS` is now ONE block, 08:03-20:33 (owner rule, 2026-09-21), so the 4h floor allows three sends a day and a 24h lead can reach its four again when it writes early enough; a night still costs a touch. The knob is still `CONTACT_WINDOWS`, and the helpers take a list, so a second block can come back without code changes. The count is read off the schedule everywhere (`max_followups_for` = `len(followup_offsets_for(lead))`), so the UI chip, the dashboard due-list, the LLM prompt and cron retirement all follow it without being told.
- **The follow-up SCRIPTS are the owner's own April 2026 copy** (owner decision, 2026-09-19, reversing the 2026-09-09 contextual rewrite). `service_type`, `area`, `availability` and `complete` in `_template_message` are the owner's words, verbatim apart from " // " becoming a blank line and each dash becoming the stop or comma it stood for. That includes "We're getting booked up this week", "tight on slots", "We price the job upfront", "The price is fixed once we confirm" and "Still looking for a plumber?". **The owner chose these knowingly; do not "fix" them back**, and change them only when asked. The `owner script` cases in TEST 0 pin every line word for word. Three things were kept around the script on the owner's say-so: `project_description` keeps the contextual copy (names their job; `plan_or_visit` was offered and declined), the `ad_service` bank for bathroom-ad leads, and the `price_tiedown` bank. `{service}` is the service TYPE in words ("bathroom renovation", else "plumbing project"), never free text, and "around Harare" is the tenant's own `location_city`, omitted when blank. The scripts go out whole; only the MODEL's rewrite is held to them (`fit_to_template`: more questions than the script means the script goes out instead, and trailing lines after the last question are cut when the script ends on one). Note the AI path still rewrites the script on every send when DeepSeek is up, and sampled drift was about 1 in 5.
- **Audited against the live sends of 2026-09-18/19** (17 leads). Each rule below is a follow-up that really went out: (1) **the ad they clicked is read** (`ad_subject`, off `ctwa_referral` headline/body). All eleven barmak ad leads came from one BATHROOM ad and were asked "is it a bathroom, a kitchen, or a new installation?"; one replied "I thought you were selling tubs, looks like it's not" and left. They now get the `ad_service` bank ("Is it the whole bathroom you want redone, or one thing in it, like the tub or the shower?"), and the AI prompt's rephrase-the-last-question block is off for them, because it handed the model the old room question to reword. (2) **An unanswered price tie-down is the open question** (`pending_price_tiedown`, reading our own follow-ups too, so the run stays on it), asked alone and named for the thing priced, never merged into an area ask ("Which suburb are you in? Also, does the starting price range sit alright with your budget?"). It keeps the ladder's signature, so a "no" to it climbs the budget ladder. (3) **One question, and the touch ends on it** (`end_on_first_question`, applied to every send in `_process_lead`), which also cuts the model's closing filler. (4) **Not every conversation is a lead** (`not_a_lead_reason`, asked in `_is_ready_for_followup`): a vendor pitching us (`is_inbound_sales_pitch`) and an `[OOS_PENDING] category=out_of_scope` message are not chased; a known job always is. (5) **Shona leads get Shona** (`lead_writes_shona`, AI-primary, since the keyword detector needs two markers; only the AI path writes Shona, the offline templates are still English). (6) **Copy that contradicted the conversation is gone**: "before I can get you a price" to a lead already priced, "earlier in the week or later?", "What time works?" to a lead with a time set, and a paragraph of description pasted in as the job (`job` falls back to the service label past six words). A description now answers the service question, as `_job_is_known` does in the chat. Also fixed on the way: `extraction_mixin` stored the classifier's display label "Bathroom Renovation" raw because its check was case-sensitive, so every reader keyed on `bathroom_renovation` missed it; `_service_label` tolerates the rows already stored. Pinned by the `followup audit` cases in TEST 0. **Still open:** the opener an ad lead gets ("Hello, How may we assist you on plumbing services", `COLD_OPENER`) ignores the ad too, and that is where these conversations stall.
- Placement, unchanged except where the floor now truncates it. On a full 72h window the touches take the band placement: **2 in the first 24h, 1 in 24-48h, 1 in 48-72h**, measured from the lead's last message — `FOLLOWUP_BAND_OFFSETS` as absolute hours per temperature (COLD 6/13/33/60h, VERY_HOT 4/10/27/51h), with `FOLLOWUP_BANDS` the contract in data and TEST 0 asserting every tuple against it. **Temperature moves a touch WITHIN its band, never into another one.** **Only a 72h window can carry that placement** — a touch written for 33h could only ever bounce with 131047 on a window that shut at 24h — so any shorter window (a standard lead, or an ad lead who replied with most of the 72h spent) gets `SHORT_WINDOW_FRACTIONS` instead: the same four touches as a share of the usable span (24h COLD ≈ 3.6/8.6/13.5/18.9h, 48h left ≈ 7.4/17.7/27.9/39.1h). Fractions rather than a second hour table, so one branch covers every window length. `followup_offsets_for(lead)` is the resolver and picks the branch on `bands[-1] <= usable_window_hours`; **the count is read off it** — `max_followups_for` is `len(followup_offsets_for(lead))`, so cron retirement, the UI chip, the dashboard due-list and the LLM prompt cannot disagree with the schedule, and `FOLLOWUP_MIN_COUNT` = 4 is the written run length, which `space_offsets` may now truncate; the nudge loops read their own `len(offsets)` rather than "at least four". Absolute offsets mean a touch deferred by the nightly contact window cannot push the later ones past the close — the old cumulative cadence (COLD 4+6+6+6 = 22h + jitter) overshot the 24h window and silently retired leads on three. **A reply resets the counter to zero** (`Appointment.reset_followup_sequence`, called from `mark_customer_response` and from the emailed-reply stamp in `process_inbound_emails`): the run is four touches SINCE THEY LAST SPOKE, so a lead who answers is never retired, and a reply revives one the cron already retired since it reopens the window too. It sets fields only — the caller saves. `last_followup_sent` is deliberately NOT cleared: it is the durable "have we ever chased this lead" record (the webhook's `mid_conversation` check reads it, since the counter no longer survives a reply). **Ad leads no longer get a bigger COUNT** — the old six-touch `CTWA_FOLLOWUP_OFFSETS` / `has_extended_window` path is gone; the longer window buys spacing, not extra messages. **Placement is reconciled with SENDABLE hours** — the messaging window intersected with `CONTACT_WINDOWS` (08:03-20:33, one window, every day, half-open so the last send is 20:32, SAST; owner rule 2026-09-21, replacing the 2026-09-06 blocks 12:33-14:33 and 16:02-19:33; the TEST 0 window cases derive from `CONTACT_WINDOWS` rather than retyping it, and `plan_quote.in_contact_window` reads the same constant, so the owner moves these hours in ONE place): the sendable span no longer shortens the SCHEDULE (`_sendable_hours` and `MIN_SENDABLE_SPAN_HOURS` are gone — shrinking the span dropped a whole touch where moving it would do), instead `_scheduled_due_at` rolls a due moment forward out of the quiet hours and, when that roll would land after the window has shut, pulls it BACK to `_last_sendable_moment` minus `LAST_CALL_GRACE_MINUTES` (`_window_moment_before` is the mirror of `_next_window_open`). Without that pull-back a touch due at 02:00 on a window closing at 06:00 waited for 08:21, by which time free-form sending was dead — so it only reached the lead when they messaged again, arriving as a stale nudge on top of their live message. A last call no longer relaxes the spacing (`LAST_CALL_MIN_GAP_HOURS` == the floor): what gives in that stretch is the TOUCH, dropped rather than squeezed. Two guards keep a touch off a live exchange: `FOLLOWUP_LIVE_CONVERSATION_MINUTES` (the lead just wrote — the bot's own reply is the touch; this also replaces the old 2-minute eligibility prefilter) and `FOLLOWUP_QUIET_AFTER_OUTBOUND_HOURS` (we just wrote). The delay-ghost and parked nudge loops share the last-call rule via `_is_last_call`. Delay-flow and parked nudges use the same rule (`_DELAY_NUDGE_FRACTIONS`, `_PARKED_NUDGE_FRACTIONS`), go through the same `space_offsets`, and are capped by the same cross-loop ceiling — parked nudges moved from 3/7 DAYS (always outside the window, so every one bounced 131047) into the back half of the window. A closed window still blocks everything: we never pay for templates. Other Railway crons: `send_reminders`, `send_job_reminders`, `summarize_unconfirmed_leads`, `process_inbound_emails`, `send_scheduled_followups`. **Every service in the Railway project shares the repo's `railway.json`, whose `deploy.startCommand` overrides each service's own** — so all of them boot `start.sh`, which runs `manage.py <job>` when `PLUMBOT_CRON` is set (comma-separated for a service carrying several jobs) and gunicorn when it isn't. Adding a cron = new service + `cronSchedule` + a `PLUMBOT_CRON` value, never a start command in the dashboard (it would be overridden). The `_cronsReference` block in `railway.json` documents the current mapping (services: `Follow_Ups`, `Reminders`, `Email_Follow_Ups`, and the `Plumbot_v1` web role); Railway does not read it. **A Railway deployment bakes in the cron schedule the service had when that deployment was created.** One built while the schedule was absent comes out `buildOnly=false` / `cronSchedule=null`: it runs `start.sh` once at deploy time and never ticks again, while the service page still shows the schedule and Railway keeps advancing `nextCronRunAt` — silent, with the deployment sitting at SUCCESS. That stopped `send_followups` dead from 2026-08-25 to 2026-08-28; the only follow-ups sent in between came from a human pressing the dashboard's manual follow-up check (`manual_followup_check`), which runs the command inside the WEB service. `railway redeploy` does not fix it — it replays the bad snapshot; re-apply the schedule (`serviceInstanceUpdate`) then deploy fresh (`serviceInstanceDeployV2`). To check a cron service is genuinely alive, read its latest deployment meta (`buildOnly` true, `serviceManifest.deploy.cronSchedule` set) and confirm its `deploymentLogs` carry one `[start.sh] cron job` line per tick — the DB alone cannot tell a dead cron from a quiet one. See `_cronScheduleTrap` in `railway.json`. (`notify_priority_leads` — the daily plumber WhatsApp alert — was removed 2026-07-08; the priority-leads dashboard pages remain.)

### The plumber handoff and the job-date ladder (handoff brief, owner 2026-09-21)
- **The plumber's own line is offered as a `wa.me` link the LEAD taps** (`bot/plumber_link.py`). The plumber runs on a separate WhatsApp Business number with no template protection, so he must never open a cold thread: every conversation there starts inbound. The link carries a pre-filled message in the lead's voice (service type, area, one line of description, then the FIXED "I can send measurements, photos, and a plan" line), built by interpolation and URL-encoded, **no model call**. A thin field drops its sentence. The number is `plumber_contact()` (per-lead override, then the lead's own tenant); none means no offer, never another tenant's number. The customer paragraph is `copy_catalog.PLUMBER_QUOTE_OFFER`, in WE voice ("message us directly"), English only. `[PLUMBER_LINK_SENT]` means it goes once per lead, whichever path sends it.
- **Rule 1, a delay to a date more than a week out** (`job_date_ladder.applies`, the complement of `_timeframe_is_near`): that date is the JOB date. The check-back stored is job - 7, and the lead is asked about it as a literal date ("Will it be okay if we follow up with you on the 14th of October? That gives you time to go over a quote..."), then the portfolio/email, as ONE turn in TWO split parts, because `finalise_outbound` keeps one question per part. "fixed" was dropped from the owner's wording because it is one tenant's promise. The link rides with the portfolio in every branch (address on file, address captured, PDF on WhatsApp). A new date in answer to the permission question re-arms the ladder instead of reading as a declined email. A week or less out keeps the old near-date rules.
- **The ladder** (`[JOB_DATE]`, `[JOB_LADDER]` step, `[JOB_LADDER_T1]`; walked by `send_followups._process_job_date_ladder`): a touch at job - 7 and job - 3 (email when we have one, else WhatsApp only when the free window is open; counted by the four-touch cap via `[JOB DATE FOLLOW-UP]`), and at job - 2, if the lead has not written since the first touch, the plumber is emailed a CALL BRIEF: the lead's details and the owner's call script, routed to B1 (a quote was SENT) or B2 (none) automatically. A reply after the first touch closes the ladder; a booking takes the lead off it; a cron back from an outage fires only the latest due step. Steps advance even when no channel could carry a touch, because for a no-email lead with a shut window the call is the only reach left. `_process_delayed_reactivations` skips ladder leads (their stored check-back IS job - 7), except the near-term `[DELAY_KIND]` check-ins. Pinned by `job ladder` in TEST 0 and `bot/test_handoff.py`.
- **Rule 2, a qualified lead who goes quiet**: the SECOND follow-up since their last reply (`Command.HANDOFF_ATTEMPT`) is the plumber link with the same offer, once service type, description and area are all in ('other' is not a service type). The first touch is still the owner's script. Pinned by `plumber link: ghosted` in TEST 0.
- **Not built yet** from the same brief: the price-objection line in the post-quote follow-up email (the brief flags it as unconfirmed), and the three edge cases (price-shock silence after a quote, a tenant needing landlord sign-off, the day-25 win-back on an expiring quote). The quote-to-booking sequence it asks for already exists (`start_quote_followups`, see post-visit-and-plan-path.md).

### Cancelling: a scheduled appointment, and a lead's follow-ups
- **`[FOLLOWUPS_OFF]` is the staff stop, and it is one tag read by every proactive send path.** The controls existed only on the lead's own page, and the one that claimed to stop the AUTOMATION - `pause_auto_followup` - wrote `manual_followup_paused` / `manual_followup_paused_until`, **columns migration 0018 REMOVED**, so it set plain Python attributes `save()` never persisted: the button reported success and stopped nothing (it was pinned as an `expectedFailure` for exactly that). It now writes a lead-level tag alongside the ones already there, added to `post_visit.SUPPRESSED_TAGS` (which `plan_quote` and `visit_proposal` extend) and to `send_followups._exclude_suppressed_states` plus the two nudge loops' own exclude chains - **no new column and no new resolver**, because the decision has to live on the LEAD or every send path re-litigates it. Deliberately NOT `[STOP_REQUESTED]`: that one means the CUSTOMER asked us to stop, is never cleared automatically and also sets `is_lead_active=False`, while this is our own decision and the same staff member puts it back (`Appointment.pause_followups` / `resume_followups` / `followups_paused`). **No duration** - a timed pause needs a column, and the ask was "stop, and I will put it back". The bot still answers if they write; only the proactive sends stop. Resuming fires no burst: the schedule is measured from the lead's last message, so a lead who went quiet is already spent. `get_upcoming_emails` marks a paused lead's unsent rows `stopped` rather than dropping them - they are what Restart would put back - and the dashboard's upcoming/overdue lists read the status, so they fall out of both.
- **Cancel is POST, everywhere, and it returns you to the screen you pressed it on.** `cancel_appointment` was a plain `<a href>`, which a browser can prefetch; it is `@require_POST` now and the detail page posts through the surrounding form's CSRF token via `formaction`, the same trick Complete and Delete use (a nested `<form>` there would be invalid HTML). `_followup_redirect` and `cancel_appointment` both honour a validated `next` through `safe_return_path` (the quote views' own resolver - only the local PATH survives, so a value that reached the form cannot redirect off-site), because raising or stopping something from the diary or the follow-ups dashboard and landing on a lead page you were not reading loses your tab, window and filters.
- **A queued row is cancelled; a projected one can only be STOPPED.** The follow-ups dashboard's Upcoming lists mix two things: `ScheduledFollowup` rows a person typed and queued, and touches the automation PROJECTS from lead state (the WhatsApp schedule, the post-visit / plan-quote / visit-proposal sequences), which are not rows and have nothing to cancel one at a time. So the row carries `id` when it is a real row and the template branches on it: `fd_row_cancel.html` for a queued row, `fd_row_stop_followups.html` otherwise, and the latter is also on every lead row in the Leads tab and on the lead's own WhatsApp follow-ups tab as the master switch. **Each control's `<form>` is a SIBLING of the row's `<a>`, never a child** - a form inside an anchor is invalid HTML and the browser unnests it, dropping the button out of the row - hence the `.fd-rowwrap` / `.pb-sched-line` flex wrappers, which also carry the margin the row used to have. Pinned by `CancelControlsTests`.
