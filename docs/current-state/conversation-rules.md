The copy and flow conventions every customer-facing reply follows: the free visit and the fee said once, the availability ask, "anytime", the area fallback, advancing the sale, no emojis, no dashes.

### Convention: The opener asks what needs doing and how many rooms, one line, from the ad.
- **First contact gets the owner's opener (2026-09-22), and every first-contact path sends it** (`response_mixin.build_cold_opener`, words in `copy_catalog.OPENER_*`). "Hello, How may we assist you on plumbing services" was answered by the ad's "Can I get more info on this?" and 10 of the last 40 leads never replied; it was hardcoded in `_answer_standalone_question`'s greeting short-circuit, which is the path production took, while a newer opener existed on other paths only. The opener is one line, human (no "thanks for reaching out", no list of what we supply), assumes no project, and asks for the job description plus a quantity so multiple en-suites and new builds read it as meant for them: **"Hi, sure. What needs doing, and is it one bathroom or a few?"**. "Sure" only when they asked for info (`_INFO_ASK`), else "Hi, what needs doing, …". **Contextual per ad, no manual setup:** `ad_room` reads the click-to-WhatsApp data WhatsApp sends with an ad lead (`ctwa_referral` headline, body and the Ads Manager welcome message); bathroom words with no kitchen or other trade item name the bathroom, and anything else (a kitchen or catalog ad, no ad data) gets the fallback "one room or a few", so a wrong guess never names the wrong room. No price, one question. **The job TYPE is worked out from the description**, and "bathroom or kitchen?" is asked only when the answer does not make it clear, so the questions drop from four to three (what needs doing, area, when). **After the opener, what they say is the answer to it** (`_last_assistant_was_opener`): a product named in reply is never routed as "do you have X?" ("Two bathrooms, new tubs and showers" had been answered "Is a shower cubicle the only thing…?"), the service-inquiry step never prices it unless they asked a figure (`asks_figure`, deterministic), and the description keeps their own words, room count included, instead of the product list. Pinned by the `opener:` and `cold opener:` cases in TEST 0 and `scenarios/ad_more_info_opener.txt`. The Shona lines are a first draft for the owner to check.

### Convention: A contact card is read, and "contact them" goes straight to the plumber.
- **Owner rule, 2026-09-22** (`bot/shared_contact.py`). A card used to get NO reply: the unsupported-media handler used a lead it never looked up, crashed, and the except swallowed it. Now `handle_contacts_message` records the card as the lead's own turn ("[Sent contact] Tendai Moyo +263…") and queues it through the text batching window, so "call my husband" sent with it is read in the SAME turn whichever arrived first. `handle_turn` (early in `_generate_and_schedule_reply`, after the hard stop): card + an instruction (`asks_us_to_contact`: call / contact / speak to + him / her / them / my husband…, "he will explain", Shona "mufonerei", "taurai naye") emails the plumber a call script AT ONCE (`send_brief`, owner's call-email layout via `call_brief.build_call_email`: script first with the lead's name, the job and the area worked in, visit first, online only if hesitant, details last, Log the call button) and the lead hears `CONTACT_WILL_CALL` ("Thanks, we'll give Tendai a call."). A card alone gets `CONTACT_ASK`; a yes or an instruction next turn briefs, a no gets `CONTACT_KEPT`, anything else carries on (the ask clears whatever they say). A later "contact him" briefs the latest card once. Kept as internal_notes tags `[SHARED_CONTACT]`, `[CONTACT_ASK_PENDING]`, `[CONTACT_BRIEF_SENT]`. The subject starts "Please call today:", so the 24-hour reminder covers it. Voice notes get `VOICE_NOTE_ASK` plus what we were waiting on (`_voice_note_topic`). Pinned by `bot/test_shared_contact.py`, `bot/test_media_notices.py` and the `contact card:` cases in TEST 0.

### Convention: The free visit is said ONCE, at the start.
- **The free visit is said ONCE, at the start.** "free site visit" / "free assessment" / "free of charge" was asserted in ~77 copy sites and landed in almost every reply, follow-up and reminder; said once it is the USP that earns the appointment, said six times it reads as pleading and drags a lead who has already accepted the visit back onto cost. `strip_repeat_free_visit(reply, appointment, message)` de-qualifies it at the SAME outbound choke point as `strip_free_visit_claims` (so it catches the LLM paths, not just the hardcoded copy): the first reply that pitches the visit goes out untouched, every later one loses the claim. **The pitch survives — only the price claim comes off**, so "a free on-site visit" becomes "an on-site visit" (article fixed) rather than the sentence being dropped, which is the opposite of the fee stripper's rule and deliberately so: there a false promise is the worst outcome, here a deleted pitch is. Shapes handled: adjective, leading clause ("The visit is free — our plumber will…" → the rest is promoted), trailing clause, adverbial ("free on/when/at…"), a label parenthetical ("(Free site visit)"), Shona `mahara`, and a guard that never leaves a sentence hanging on its verb. `asks_visit_cost` is the override — a lead who asks what the visit costs gets the straight answer however often they ask (customer's own words beat the gate), and it reads the FAQ's own `free_quote` triggers via `faq.visit_cost_triggers()` so the two can't drift. `_visit_fact_line` drops "is free" from the LLM grounding fact once it's been said, so the model isn't fighting the stripper. The cron commands call the same resolver through `dequalify_free_visit(lead, message)` — applied where the message is composed, so `--dry-run` previews what actually goes out. `freestanding` / `free-standing` is guarded everywhere: it is not a price claim.

### Convention: A tenant who CHARGES for the visit follows the same once-only rule.
- **A tenant who CHARGES for the visit follows the same once-only rule.** `strip_free_visit_claims` used to append `visit_cost_sentence()` to every reply that mentioned the visit at all, so a fee tenant's lead read "The call-out to quote is US$20." on all five turns, including after they had booked — the same complaint as the repeated "free", with a harder number. It now states the fee on the first visit-related reply and after that only when `asks_visit_cost(message)` says they asked (the choke point threads `message_body` in; the param is optional so other callers are untouched). `_visit_fee_already_stated` matches the fee SENTENCE, never the bare figure — "US$20" is also a fixture price, and a false "already told them" would mean the fee never got stated at all. **The safety guarantee is unchanged and does not depend on this:** the free claim is still dropped from every reply, so a fee tenant can never be made to look free whether or not the figure is repeated. Inert today — `consultation_fee` defaults to None and no tenant sets one.

### Convention: The availability ask offers a DAY AND A TIME in one message
- **The availability ask offers a DAY AND A TIME in one message**, e.g. "Great, what works better for you, tomorrow at 9am or this Sunday at 2pm, for us to come through and have a quick look at the bathroom space?" Asking for the day alone cost a whole turn: the lead said "Sunday", we came back with "9am or 2pm?", and a booking that could have closed in one reply needed two, with a gap in between for the lead to go quiet. A lead who picks a slot is done - `_time_confirmed` reads the hour off `scheduled_datetime`, so `availability_time` never fires; a lead who answers with a bare day still gets the time question, unchanged. **`_get_two_visit_slots` (availability_mixin) is the resolver for WHICH slots** and **`_visit_slot_labels` (response_mixin) for how we say them** - one pair of resolvers because five places ask this question (the first-pass script in both of its copies, `_get_pricing_followup_prompt`'s forward question, the DeepSeek retry instruction and `_get_soft_booking_nudge`), and a day offered with a time in one of them and without in another reads as two different businesses. The two offers deliberately differ in TIME as well as day, so a lead who can only do afternoons has something to say yes to; a single open day offers two times on that day rather than one take-it-or-leave-it slot; and **no free time falls back to day-only labels, never an invented slot**. Times are written the way they get typed into WhatsApp (`_clock_label`: "9am", "2pm", "2:30pm", never "09:00AM"). The Shona joiner is "na" ("mangwana na9am"), the pattern the reschedule copy already uses; note the Shona line still takes its DAY word from `_format_day`, which is English-only - pre-existing, and the one loose end here. Pinned by the `availability ask` cases in TEST 0, including that the new sentence is still recognised by `asks_visit_availability` (a question that resolver cannot see is a call-out fee the lead never gets told).

### Convention: "Anytime is fine" is an ANSWER, and it BOOKS.
- **"Anytime is fine" is an ANSWER, and it BOOKS.** A lead who hands us the choice has already said yes to the visit, and nothing was capturing that: the `all_day_phrases` guard required a day to ALREADY be on file and the question to have moved past `availability_date` - the state the OLD two-step ask (day first, then time) was in, and one the day+time ask never reaches. So "anytime is fine" stored nothing, `next_question` stayed `availability_date`, and the retry machinery re-asked it: paraphrase, then "When would work best for you?" twice, then at retry 4 `_build_human_handoff_reply`, which calls `mark_handed_off` and **silences every follow-up path** - measured over six turns with `scheduled_datetime` None throughout, on a lead who had agreed. The unified classifier was making it worse by instruction: `"available all day" / "anytime" / "whole day" -> null` dropped the only signal there was. **Now DeepSeek JUDGES the free hand and deterministic code PICKS the slot**, which is the house split: `datetime_flexible` on the unified call (true only when they name no day and no time at all - "anytime tomorrow" names tomorrow, so it stays false and the existing date-then-time path handles it) plus a fifth `no_preference` intent in `_classify_availability_response` for the turns the unified call missed. `uc_datetime_flexible` returns **None when the model said nothing**, never False, so a dead API degrades to the phrase list instead of reading as a definite "they named a day". **`lead_has_no_time_preference` is THE resolver** (AI-primary, keyword fallback) and replaced the two copied `all_day_phrases` lists that had already drifted apart; **`resolve_flexible_slot` is THE slot picker** - the day they named if any, else the first slot we would have offered (so never a day the tenant is shut), and the hour from NOON onwards, because a lead with no preference is not sent an 8am slot and midday leaves the morning for jobs booked to a time. A full day rolls to the next working day; **nothing free returns None and the bot ASKS, never invents a slot**. Extraction takes the slot (so the normal STEPS 5 & 6 booking runs and there is ONE booking path, one confirmation, one plumber alert) and marks `_slot_was_our_choice` for the turn - per-turn only, never a column - so the reply **says the slot back** ("Perfect, let's say tomorrow at 12pm then."): the lead does not know what we picked, and the written-up confirmation is queued 45-90s later. `_name_ask_after_booking` is now the one copy of the post-booking name ask for all three paths in `generate_response` that book (five OTHER copies of that sentence remain on untouched paths). Pinned by the `any time` cases in TEST 0 and `FlexibleAvailabilityTests`, plus `scenarios/any_time_is_an_answer.txt` and `any_time_in_their_own_words.txt`.

### Convention: A part of the week is an ANSWER, not a vague reply
- **A part of the week is an ANSWER, not a vague reply** (`availability_mixin.week_part_of`, `ResponseMixin._week_part_reply`). "Let's make a date midweek" stored nothing, so the availability question came round again as a retry and the model paraphrased it into "earlier midweek or later?", a second vague question about the range the lead had just given (barmak 1162, 2026-09-19). Now "midweek" / "middle of the week" / "pakati pevhiki" (Tue to Thu), "early in the week" (Mon, Tue) and "later in / end of the week" (Thu, Fri), plus "next week" to skip this one, are read deterministically. The reply says the range back and gives the scripted ask with both slots inside it ("Midweek works for us. What works better for you, this Tuesday at 9am or this Wednesday at 2pm, ...?"). The range reaches `_get_next_two_available_days` as the per-turn `_week_part` attribute, never a column, cleared in a `finally`. A named day wins over a range, and a range the tenant is shut for falls back to the plain scan rather than offering nothing. Pinned by the `week part:` cases in TEST 0.

### Convention: DeepSeek WRITES the availability ask when the diary is an awkward shape
- **DeepSeek WRITES the availability ask when the diary is an awkward shape** (`bot/availability_ask.py`, owner request 2026-09-17). Two free slots on two days is the scripted case and stays scripted - the first ask of an early-flow question is hardcoded because consistency on first contact converts, and it must never be at the mercy of an API call on the hottest path. The OTHER shapes had no script, and the two literals standing in for one were both wrong: `slot_a = slots[0] if len(slots) > 0 else "tomorrow"` / `slot_b = slots[1] if len(slots) > 1 else "the day after"`, so **one free slot went out as "tomorrow at 9am or the day after"** - a real slot ORed with a vague phrase carrying no time, so picking the second option costs the extra turn the day+time ask exists to save - and **nothing free went out as "tomorrow or the day after"**, two invented days that ignore whether the tenant is even open then, which is the exact fault `visit_slots` was written to prevent. Two slots sharing a day read "tomorrow at 9am or tomorrow at 2pm". The same literals were in the RETRY INSTRUCTION too, so a busy diary handed the model a day nobody had checked and it wrote that day in its own words. **`_availability_ask` is now THE builder** (the first-pass script and its legacy copy both go through it), `_ask_needs_composing` decides whether the shape has a script, and `_scripted_availability_ask` is the deterministic sentence - **correct for every shape on its own, because it is what goes out when the composer is off, down, or rejected**: one slot offers that one slot, a shared day says the day once, nothing free asks openly and names no day at all, and Shona joins with "kana" rather than "or". **The model NEVER chooses a slot**: code resolves them and hands them over as fixed strings, and `fences_hold` REJECTS any composition that names a clock time or a day word we did not supply, carries a figure or a promise word, asks other than exactly one question, or runs past 220 characters; a rejection is logged and the scripted sentence goes out. It **fails open** on timeout, bad JSON or a refusal. **The opening hours are deliberately NOT given to the model**: that sentence carries day names and clock times ("Sunday to Friday, 8am to 6pm"), so a fence loose enough to pass it would pass an invented "Sunday" on an empty diary - the caller appends the tenant's hours afterwards. `PLUMBOT_ASK_COMPOSER=0` turns the whole thing off without a deploy. Fixed on the way, in BOTH this module and `response_check`: the money regex matched the rand prefix `R` case-insensitively with no left boundary, so **"or 2pm" and "for 20 minutes" read as figures** - a sound question was rejected as having named a price. Pinned by the `ask shape` / `ask copy` / `ask fence` / `ask retry` cases in TEST 0 and `ComposedAvailabilityAskTests`.

### Convention: The visit price is stated ONCE, with the AVAILABILITY ASK, and it carries its own close.
- **The visit price is stated ONCE, with the AVAILABILITY ASK, and it carries its own close.** Not the opener: a fee quoted before the lead knows what they are buying prices the job, while the same sentence at the moment we ask for a day answers the question they are about to ask anyway. The approved wording is "[Great, ]we can come for a quick site visit. There's a US$X call-out fee which covers the visit and we'll do a full check for you.[ NEWLINE If you decide to go ahead with the <service type>, we'll take that cost off the final price.] Want me to book you a time?" — **plain words on purpose** ("full diagnosis" is trade language) with the waiver as its OWN sentence, so it reads as an offer rather than a caveat hanging off the fee (`TenantConfig.visit_price_note(is_shona, opening, job_noun)`; `opening` adds the warm-up word only when the note leads the message, `job_noun` is the lead's OWN service type via `_visit_job_noun`, read back from `PROJECT_TYPE_CHOICES` the way it was offered to them, so the waiver names the thing they actually asked for: "the geyser repair", "the bathroom renovation". It degrades to "repair" then "work" while the type is unset or 'other', which is the only honest thing to say before they have told us; Shona keeps the two plain nouns rather than bending English trade labels. Note underscores are word characters, so the repair test normalises them or "geyser_repair" never matches `repair`). **It is its own turn, after the area answer and before the availability question.** `strip_free_visit_claims` no longer volunteers the figure at all: it used to speak whenever a dropped free claim would leave a hole, which fired on the AREA question (the Shona quote pitch says "mahara", the claim is dropped for a fee tenant, and "The call-out to quote is US$10" was stapled onto "What area are you in?" — prod, Barmak, 2026-09-03 18:57). Worse, having been stated there, the note then saw the price already stated and never fired, so the lead met the figure at the worst possible moment and never got the offer that makes sense of it. It now speaks ONLY when the lead asks; dropping the false promise stays unconditional, so safety never depended on saying it. `asks_visit_availability` is the deterministic trigger and **the note REPLACES that question** (`_question_offset` finds it) rather than sitting in front of it: two questions in one reply breaks the house rule and the lead answers only the last one anyway. Whatever the reply said BEFORE the question is kept. Runs LAST at the choke point.

### Convention: The lock-in close is a yes/no, so BOTH answers are handled.
- **The lock-in close is a yes/no, so BOTH answers are handled.** `_handle_no_to_lock_in` (routed in the webhook beside the other no-handlers, before the slot-offer one) answers a refusal without ever re-sending the close or restating the figure. The no is ambiguous in a way a day/time no never is, because the message it refuses just named a fee: it can mean "not at that price" or "not right now". So it ISOLATES rather than guesses, in one question with two options, and **the fee only appears as an option for a tenant who charges one** — offering "or is it the call-out fee?" to a lead told the call-out is free invents an objection they never had. `_declines_lock_in` is deliberately wider than `_is_bare_negative` ("no thanks", "not yet", "maybe later" are how people decline a yes/no close) but capped at five words, so "no thanks, I got someone to do it Friday" keeps its information. A HIGH delay signal ("not right now") is handed BACK to the delay flow, which parks the lead with a real check-back date instead.

### Convention: The customer's own words override any gate or holding state.
- **Context over the script** (owner rule, 2026-09-21: "Contextual response should always be put above everything ... do not just keep looping the delay signal messages"). Four rules. (1) **A scripted draft that repeats something we recently said is read in context**: `finalise_outbound` turns the model reader (`response_check`) back ON for a `check=False` draft when `utils.repeats_recent_reply` finds it the same as, inside, or 85% alike one of our last 4 SENT messages; a FIRST ask keeps the reader off, because that is where it used to tidy the timeframe and email asks away. (2) **The controller's proof step (photos) stands aside for a lead who has said "not yet"**: a delay signal in this message (`uc_intent`, `_is_explicit_deferral`), a delay-flow step waiting on them (`in_delay_flow`), or an agreed check-back still ahead (`has_agreed_checkback`: `delay_followup_due_at` or an armed job-date ladder, because the delay TAG is cleared on every inbound by `_clear_delay_signal_if_present` and so cannot answer this). It also stands aside for an out-of-area lead. It answered "I'm in Chitungwiza, I'll get back to you once I've sorted the money" with photos and "Want me to book you a time?". (3) **"Send it here" from a deferred lead is about the portfolio, not photos**, and once the PDF is already in the chat (`[LEAD_MAGNET_WA_SENT]`) STEP 0-a answers that (`copy_catalog.PORTFOLIO_ALREADY_HERE`, sent through the reader so it fits their words) instead of the next scripted step, which offered visit slots. (4) **The area backfill runs BEFORE the proof step** (with the language normalisation it reads): the proof step answers and returns, so "In shurugwi" was never stored (barmak 1231) and the question sent with the photos was picked as if the area were missing. And **the controller may reorder the flow's questions, never add one**: `controller.choose_question` refuses `timeline` off the plan path and `name` before a booking, so a lead with the job and the area gets the booking question, not "When were you hoping to get started?". Pinned by `repeat guard` and `question choice` in TEST 0, `TwoFollowupsAndSilenceTests`, and `scenarios/area_then_booking_not_timeline.txt` / `email_ask_gives_a_reason.txt`.
- **A batched "area + I need prices" turn gets the tenant's own pricing, not a location answer and a Homebase price list** (barmak 1231, 2026-09-21: "In shurugwi" + "I need prices first" got "We're based in Harare." and a hardcoded line of HOMEBASE fixture prices ending "Which of these do you need?", to a lead who had said "the whole bathroom"). Offline the stub's service detector returns an empty intent, so the scenario cannot show the overview itself; its rejects pin what must never come back.
- **Chat is never stored as the job** (2026-09-21). "Ok / Now you are talking", two taps reacting to our price breakdown, was saved as lead 1217's `project_description` (barmak, 2026-09-20) an hour after they had described the real job, and the plumber-link message then quoted it back in the lead's own voice. A filled description means the flow never asks again, so one bad write loses the real one. The leak was `_looks_like_project_description_reply`'s three-word fallback, whose no-content list lacked "talking". **One rule now, `bot/job_text.describes_a_job`** (at least one word outside `CONTENTLESS_WORDS`: acknowledgements, time talk, sentence glue, and reactions to what we said), read in three places: that gate; **`Appointment.save`**, the net under all nine writers of the field (extractor, raw fallbacks, webhook early capture, dashboard), which drops a chat-only description whenever a save writes the field; and `plumber_link`. ### Convention: The AREA has a deterministic fallback, because the classifier was the only thing that had 
- **The AREA has a deterministic fallback, because the classifier was the only thing that had ever written one.** All three area writes — the webhook's early capture, `extraction_mixin`'s passive capture and `process_extracted_data` — read `area` off the classifier and none had a fallback, so ANY classifier failure empties the field in all three at once, silently. That is what happened to a lead who answered "Bluffhill." and "Need to renovate my bathroom" in two taps (the debounce delivers them as ONE message): area never stored, the flow stuck on the area question, the lead asked about a job they had just described, and **the visit never pitched** (prod, barmak, 2026-09-18). **The extraction itself was NOT the weak link, and this was measured, not assumed** — 28 messages x 3 reps through `unified_turn` called the way production calls it scored 100% on area/description/availability/name, including seven batched "suburb + job detail" variants, and the legacy `extract_all_available_info_with_ai` gets them too. So do NOT "fix" this class of bug by enriching the classifier prompt: there is no measured deficit there, and adding tokens to the hottest prompt in the system buys nothing. What is left as the real cause is a call that returns **None** (a parse failure or timeout nulls every field at once and no caller can tell that from "not present" — there is no per-field confidence), a branch that answers and RETURNS before extraction, or a stale `Appointment` instance overwriting the save. **The fallback is a floor under all three, because it never consults the classifier.** `ResponseMixin._area_from_reply` is THE resolver, read by both capture paths, and `_we_just_asked_the_area` is the gate — only the LATEST assistant turn counts, since an area ask three turns back has been answered or abandoned. It **invents nothing**: no place in the message means None and we ask again, because a wrong suburb closes the question with a lie. Three things it must never file as a suburb, each with its own guard: job talk (`_AREA_REJECT_MARKERS`), acknowledgements (`_AREA_NON_ANSWERS`, matched per TOKEN so "ok thanks" is caught), and an AVAILABILITY answer — "whenever suits you" carries no job word and no chat word, so what stops it is the rule that **a place name contributes at least one word that is not ordinary chat** (`_AREA_COMMON_WORDS` / `_AREA_TEMPORAL_WORDS`), plus a 3-word cap. The locative prefix is stripped only behind `ndiri`/`tiri`, or a bare "pa" eats the first two letters of "Parktown". This mirrors `_keyword_availability_date`, which has been the same kind of floor under the availability field all along.

### Convention: The bot advances the sale unless the lead CLEARLY states otherwise
- **The bot advances the sale unless the lead CLEARLY states otherwise** (owner rule, 2026-09-18). `close_pleasantry` ENDS a turn, and the owner sign-offs it was lifted from all presuppose a SETTLED conversation ("All good, we'll speak on Thursday then" is said once a day is agreed). Said into a live sale the same words are not a sign-off, they are a lost lead: a lead asked the tub price, got it with the budget tie-down, replied "Ok thank you" and received "Got it, no problem." - tie-down unanswered, nothing on the diary, no question outstanding and no reason to write again (prod, 2026-09-18). **An acknowledgement is not a decision.** `controller._sale_is_open` is the gate and is the general case the two existing ones (`_asks_us_something`, `_booking_half_made`) are each a special case of; held back, the turn falls through to the deterministic router, which asks the next thing the flow needs - which is what advancing means. "Settled" REUSES `post_visit.lead_is_suppressed` rather than restating it (booked, parked, handed off, stopped, declined, out of area, job on the diary, inactive), so a new stop state cannot mean "settled" here and "still live" everywhere else. A lead who genuinely wants out is an EXIT signal with its own handling, which parks them and leaves a real check-back behind. The move itself is kept - it is the model's most-requested call and right once there is an outcome to end on.

### Convention: An acknowledgement is not a project description either.
- **An acknowledgement is not a project description either.** The word-count fallback in `_looks_like_project_description_reply` treated ANY three-word message as a description, so the same turn stored "Ok thank you" as the job - and since any non-empty string satisfies `controller.description_captured`, the bot would then never ask what the work actually is and `lead_handoff.job_phrase` reads it back as "your Ok thank you". ("Alright thank you", "thank you so much", "yes please do", "that is fine thanks" all did the same.) A message made entirely of words that carry no content is an acknowledgement, whatever its length: `_CONTENTLESS_WORDS` is the shared union of the three vocabularies the area resolver already uses, named neutrally because the judgement is not area-specific. The length fallback still earns its place for a real description carrying no marker word ("the one in the corner"). Pinned by the `ack is not a description` / `advance the sale` cases in TEST 0, `AdvanceTheSaleTests` (bot/test_views_actions.py) and `scenarios/ack_does_not_end_a_live_sale.txt`.

### Convention: Benchmarking the classifier: pass `today_date`, or you will chase a bug that is your harne
- **Benchmarking the classifier: pass `today_date`, or you will chase a bug that is your harness.** `unified_turn` takes it and the webhook always supplies it; omit it and the model has no anchor for a weekday, so "Thursday morning" comes back as a date MONTHS in the past and reads as a serious extraction defect. With it passed, every weekday form resolves correctly ("Thursday morning", "next Thursday at 9am", "neChina mangwanani", "Sunday at 2pm"). Same for appointment state: the prompt summarises the row, so a benchmark whose fake lead already holds an area measures a different question from the one you meant.

### Convention: The classifier NORMALISES, and the normalised label was failing the service-type gate.
- **The classifier NORMALISES, and the normalised label was failing the service-type gate.** "Need to renovate my bathroom" comes back as "renovate my bathroom", which IS `_is_service_type_only` and was discarded at `_desc_retry == 0` — so the lead was asked to describe a job they had just described, while their OWN sentence would have passed. That gate is about the LEAD handing us a bare category, never about the extractor tidying a real sentence into one, so when their words carry more than the category their words win. The `elif` below it already did this when the description was the question in play; the fix covers detail VOLUNTEERED while they answer something else. `_job_words_only` drops the suburb half of a batched turn on the way in, or the description reads back through `lead_handoff.job_phrase` as "your Bluffhill. Need to renovate my bathroom". Pinned by the `area reply` cases in TEST 0 and `BatchedAreaReplyTests` (bot/test_views_actions.py).

### Convention: No emojis in customer-facing copy
- **No emojis in customer-facing copy** (logs/dashboards fine). Support English + Shona.

### Convention: No dash PUNCTUATION in customer-facing copy.
- **No dash PUNCTUATION in customer-facing copy.** Nobody types an em dash on a phone, so a reply full of them reads as drafted prose rather than a text message. `utils.strip_dashes` turns a clause dash into the comma or full stop the sentence was reaching for (interrogatives and independent clauses start a new sentence, short interjections take a stop: "Perfect - thanks" becomes "Perfect. Thanks"), a range into "to" (`8am-6pm` -> `8am to 6pm`, `Sun-Fri` -> `Sun to Fri`), and a line-opening dash into a bullet. **Hyphens INSIDE words are left alone** (`on-site`, `all-in`, `wall-hung`, `call-out`, `20-minute`): stripping those would give "onsite" and "allin". Applied LAST at the outbound choke point, after the price note is appended, plus in `dequalify_free_visit` for cron copy and beside every existing `strip_emojis` call (the LLM output points). Every prompt that bans emojis now bans dashes too, so the model shapes the sentence for a comma instead of having one repaired afterwards. **Do NOT bulk-rewrite dashes in source**: an automated pass over the literals broke the hours formatter (`{a} - {b}` range templates) and the photo-description delimiter that `_describe_work_image` writes and the keyword resolver parses back. The stripper runs on the FINAL rendered message, which is the only layer where a dash is certain to be punctuation and not structure. ~75 high-traffic strings were hand-rewritten; the tail stays in source and is cleaned at output.
